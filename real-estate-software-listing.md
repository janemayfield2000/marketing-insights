<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Real estate software companies keep making the same mistake: they treat every real-estate-themed website as a marketing channel. But a renter searching for an apartment and a property manager evaluating a lease administration platform are not the same audience, and pretending otherwise wastes budget fast.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The smarter approach is separating software-buying environments from consumer property searches. Platforms like G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, GetApp, and Software Advice attract people actively comparing software &mdash; not people hunting for a two-bedroom near downtown. That distinction should shape where a proptech company spends its first wave of listing effort.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Category clarity matters just as much as platform choice. A product that calls itself a CRM on one site, a marketing suite on another, and an AI platform on a third confuses buyers and dilutes positioning. Before submitting anywhere, teams need to lock down what the product actually is.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Getting this right isn't about volume. It's about making sure the people who see your listing are actually the people who'd buy your software.</span></p>
