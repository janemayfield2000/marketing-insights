<h1 style="font-size: 32px; font-weight: 700; color: #ffffff; margin-bottom: 16px;">NFTs Didn't Fail — Trust Did</h1>
<img src="https://jpcdn.it/img/e654387a3dc12af468a9b3f058c4f30c.jpg" alt="NFTs Didn't Fail — Trust Did" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 20px 0;">

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">It's tempting to say NFTs simply "failed." But the technology worked exactly as designed. What broke down was public trust, and that distinction matters enormously for anyone building in Web3 today.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">The backlash was fueled by timing as much as substance. Most people's first exposure to NFTs came through celebrity promotions, extreme price headlines, or outright scams — not through a useful, everyday application. First impressions are sticky, and once a category gets labeled "mostly hype," every legitimate use case has to fight uphill just to be heard.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">Market volatility made things worse. Rapid pumps followed by sudden collapses reinforced the sense that ordinary participants were always at a disadvantage. Even people genuinely open to innovation grew cautious once price action started to feel disconnected from anything they could reasonably understand.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">A well-researched piece digs into exactly <a href="https://unicornplatform.com/blog/why-do-people-hate-nfts-the-psychology-behind-the-backlash/" style="color: #4da6ff; text-decoration: underline;">why so many people turned against NFTs</a> and lays out a step-by-step approach for founders trying to rebuild credibility after category-level hype. It's a useful reference if you're navigating a skeptical audience in any emerging tech space.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">Recovery is possible, but it isn't automatic. It requires acknowledging skepticism directly, being explicit about what is and isn't being sold, and showing real execution instead of promises about the future. Trust, once lost, has to be earned back one clear interaction at a time.</p>
