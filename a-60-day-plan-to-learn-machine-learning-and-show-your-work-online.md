<h1 style="font-size: 32px; font-weight: 700; color: #ffffff; margin-bottom: 16px;">A 60-Day Plan to Learn Machine Learning and Show Your Work Online</h1>

<img src="https://jpcdn.it/img/small/f7076288b9ebec7d6d63415bd8a17106.jpg" alt="A 60-Day Plan to Learn Machine Learning and Show Your Work Online" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 20px 0;">

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">Big goals feel manageable when they have a deadline and a structure. If you want to move from curiosity to visible results in machine learning, a focused 60-day plan can give you momentum without burning you out. The idea is simple: learn, build, and publish in clear phases.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">In the first fifteen days, choose your platform sequence and complete your first fundamentals modules. Set up your coding environment and start a learning log. The <a href="https://unicornplatform.com/blog/top-5-best-websites-to-learn-machine-learning-for-free/" style="color: #4da6ff; text-decoration: underline;">free machine learning courses and platforms in this guide</a> can help you pick a sequence so you do not waste early weeks deciding where to begin.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">Days sixteen to thirty are for your first project cycle. Build one small project using tabular data, and document your assumptions, your baseline model, and your results. Keep it small enough to finish, because a completed project teaches more than an ambitious one left half-done.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">From day thirty-one to forty-five, go deeper on evaluation. Practice model validation, metric interpretation, and error analysis, then improve your first project with one or two iteration cycles. This is where many learners discover that accuracy alone rarely tells the full story.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">The final stretch, days forty-six to sixty, is about going public. Publish a project summary and your key learning insights on your own website or blog, and add one clear call to action for interested readers. Sharing distilled lessons builds accountability and can attract helpful feedback.</p>

<p style="margin-bottom: 18px; font-size: 16px;">By day sixty you will not be an expert, and that is fine. You will have a documented project, a learning log, and a public record of progress. That combination is a strong foundation for whatever you choose to learn next.</p>
